01 About

“If a human can do it, so can I.”

03 Method

Prototype to production, on repeat.

  1. 01

    Prototype

    Something you can click, as early as possible.

  2. 02

    Build

    Spec first, then code and tests written against the spec.

  3. 03

    Deploy

    Containers, backups and a runbook, on whatever hosting fits.

  4. 04

    Update

    Tagged releases, one after another, for as long as it runs.

My speed through this loop is the thing no project page records, and I think it is my real strength.

The projects rarely show it, but the way I build keeps changing as fast as the tools do. I rarely do the same thing a third time the same way. Right now that means working AI-native: every repository carries its own instructions, skills and review passes for coding agents, and scheduled agents handle the routine.

On record PatrolX went from first commit to 358 commits and 48 tagged releases in six months.

05 Roots

I don't stand here alone.

  1. 2017

    A quadratic-equation solver in HTML and JavaScript. It won the notice of my maths teacher and vice principal, Sukhwani sir, at Macro Vision School, Burhanpur.

  2. 2018

    An accidental founder: I gathered a group of 9th and 10th standard students to learn iOS development, and the school sponsored it.

  3. 2022

    Co-founded AtomDrop, a 3D and game studio.

  4. 2026

    Left banking for Techformers, my own company, which solves business problems with software.

My grandfather left his village and its ways to start a life of education. My father studied in a government school and has spent his working life taking education to places that had none, through Swami Vivekanand Mahavidyalaya and its NGO, work that now reaches people with disabilities and the less privileged. As a child I rarely saw him. He left before I woke for school and came home after I was asleep.

I carry that legacy, and I carry luck: I was born a boy, in an upper-caste family, in India. Each of those words marks a divide. The privilege I have received is immense and I am grateful for it.

I believe a duty comes with standing where I stand.

I pay attention to society, politics, capitalism and philosophy because they are part of the story of the world. I can see how unevenly its resources, and its love, are shared out, and who benefits from that.
